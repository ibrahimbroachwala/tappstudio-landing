Questions you're probably asking

Why not just hire an agency?

You can. You'll also pay for their office, their sales team, and the junior developer practicing on your codebase. With me, every rupee goes toward the thing you actually asked for.

What does it cost?

Depends on scope. Tell me what you need and I'll give you a straight number, not a range wide enough to park a bus in.

How long does it really take?

About a week of build time, counted from the day scope is signed off. Settling the scope takes as long as it takes us to decide. Slow feedback during the week moves the date, and store review afterwards is up to Apple and Google.

How do I test the app before it's live?

iPhone testers get a TestFlight invite, Android testers get a Firebase App Distribution invite, and websites get a private preview link. The first build reaches your phone on Day 1, with a new one every day or two after that. I just need an Apple ID email for each iPhone tester and a Gmail address for each Android tester.

Do I need Apple and Google developer accounts to start?

No. Test builds go out through my own accounts, so there's nothing to set up and no approval to wait for. Before launch you'll need your own Apple Developer account ($99/year) and Google Play Console account ($25 once), and the app moves into them. Apple can take days to approve them, longer for companies, so create them while I build.

Do I own the code?

Yes. It lives in your repository from day one, and the app moves into your own store accounts before launch.

I only have an idea, not a spec. Is that enough?

That's usually where this starts. Scoping it into something buildable is part of the job, and it happens before the week starts.

What do you actually use AI for?

I build with Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding agent, alongside my own saved code guides: written standards and best practices I reuse on every project. So an app starts from a proven structure instead of a blank page, and boilerplate and repetitive work goes much faster. AI doesn't decide what your product should be. That part's on me, and I review every line before it ships.

What if I need changes after launch?

Normal. Apps evolve. Reasonable bugs are fixed for good, at no extra cost. New features are a separate conversation: we scope them the same way as the original, with a straight price.
