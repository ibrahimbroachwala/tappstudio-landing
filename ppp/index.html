How to use this

  1. Enter your price and the country you charge it in. ₹99 in India, $4.99 in the US, whatever you already know works.
  2. Pick the product type (subscription or one-time) and leave “Snap to App Store price points” on, so every App Store price is one Apple actually offers.
  3. Check the table. View it by tier or as one list, sort it A–Z, and nudge the tier multipliers under “Adjust tier multipliers” if you want.
  4. Copy the AI prompt. Click “Set up these prices with AI”, add your product's name, and copy it.
  5. Paste it into your coding AI (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot or similar) inside your app's repo. It builds scripts/store_pricing/pricing.yaml with every price filled in, plus a script for each store.
  6. Create your API keys (below) and save them in scripts/store_pricing/credentials/, which the AI keeps out of git.
  7. Dry run. The AI runs plan for each store. It changes nothing and writes a CSV of every country's current and new price. Read it.
  8. Apply. Run apply and type yes. Check a few countries in App Store Connect and Play Console afterwards; stores can take a little while to show new prices.

App Store Connect API key

  1. Sign in as the Account Holder or an Admin.
  2. Users and Access → Integrations → App Store Connect API → Team Keys. First time? The Account Holder clicks Request Access once.
  3. Generate a key with access App Manager.
  4. Download the .p8 file. Apple only lets you download it once. Note the Key ID and the Issuer ID shown on the same page.

Open App Store Connect keys Apple's guide

Google Play service account

  1. In Google Cloud, enable the Google Play Android Developer API.
  2. Create a service account, then add a JSON key and download it.
  3. In Play Console → Users and permissions, invite the service account's email. Give it your app with: view app information, manage store presence (products and prices), and manage orders and subscriptions if you'll move existing subscribers.
  4. New permissions can take up to a day to start working.

How the prices are worked out

Purchasing power parity (PPP) asks how much a basket of goods costs in each country compared with the US. The World Bank publishes it every year. Divide a country's PPP conversion factor by its exchange rate and you get its price level ratio: 1.00 means prices like the US, 0.25 means things cost about a quarter as much in dollar terms.

Tiers

Every country goes into one of 5 tiers by its ratio, with no hand edits. Each tier's multiplier is the median ratio of the countries in it, rounded to 0.05. Countries with no data from either source stay at full price.

The formula

  1. Your price ÷ exchange rate ÷ your country's tier multiplier ÷ (1 + its VAT) = a pre-tax Tier 1 anchor in US dollars.
  2. Each country: anchor × its tier multiplier × (1 + its VAT) × exchange rate into the currency each store bills it in.
  3. App Store: rounded to the local pattern, then the nearest real App Store price point. Google Play: rounded to the local pattern (.99, ₹99, ¥480, 1,490).

VAT is added because store prices include it everywhere except the US and Canada, so without it you'd earn less in Europe than in the US for the same tier. Turn it off if you'd rather keep the digits the same.

Sources

  • Price levels: World Bank PPP conversion factor (PA.NUS.PPP) ÷ official exchange rate (PA.NUS.FCRF), latest year with both (mostly 2025). For economies the World Bank doesn't cover, like Taiwan, or where it disagrees with the IMF World Economic Outlook by more than 20% (usually a currency change caught halfway, as with Bulgaria and the euro), the IMF figure is used. Hover a ratio to see its source.
  • Store countries and currencies: the 175 App Store territories and 174 Google Play countries, each with the currency that store bills in (they often differ: Apple bills over 100 territories in US dollars).
  • App Store price points: read from the App Store Connect API for every territory, for subscriptions and one-time purchases.
  • Exchange rates: fawazahmed0/exchange-api, free and updated daily, fetched by your browser and kept for 12 hours. If it can't be reached, a saved copy is used and the date says so.
  • VAT/GST: standard rates from node-sales-tax. Where a rate is unknown, the country is treated as tax-free.
  • Flags: flag-icons (MIT).

What to keep in mind

  • PPP data is yearly and a year behind. Exchange rates move daily. Fast-moving currencies (Turkey, Argentina, Egypt, Nigeria) are worth rechecking every few months.
  • A few small economies sit higher than their incomes suggest because imports are expensive there. That's what the data says, so I've left it alone. Adjust the multipliers if you disagree.
  • Google Play takes any price within each currency's limits; the AI's dry run flags anything outside them.
  • This is a well-reasoned starting point, not financial advice. Watch your conversion in each region and adjust.
